# Reaching out: what to ask for, and in what order

Companion to [native-plant-societies.md](native-plant-societies.md), which is
the who. This is the how.

## The three asks, easiest first

Native plant societies are volunteer-run and get pitched constantly. Ask for the
smallest thing that is still worth having.

1. **Look at it and tell us what's wrong.** A chapter's horticulture chair knows
   the regional flora better than our sources do. "Here are the 46 plants we
   list for your region — what did we get wrong?" costs them twenty minutes and
   gives us a correction list nobody else can produce. It is also the only ask
   that flatters rather than extracts.
2. **Mention it to members.** A newsletter line, a slide at a meeting, a link on
   the chapter's resources page. Only worth asking after (1), when they have
   seen the thing and it survived their scrutiny.
3. **Point new gardeners at us, and let us point them back.** The two-way one:
   Indigene answers *what do I plant here*, the chapter answers *come dig with
   us in March*. This is the ask that matters most and should be raised last.

## Who to write to

Not the president. The **Programs chair** or **Horticulture chair** — they field
"what should I plant" from the public, book speakers, and have a reason to care
about a free tool. For organizations with paid staff (Native Plant Trust, the
Backyard Habitat program, the Conservatoires botaniques nationaux, the National
Biodiversity Data Centre), write to the education or engagement lead instead.

Pull the current name from the board page the day you send. Officers turn over
annually.

## Sequencing

Don't send fifty letters. Pick three regions, learn what the objections are,
then scale.

Good first three, for different reasons:

- **Pacific Northwest** — WNPS and NPSO are large, well-organized, and the
  Backyard Habitat Certification Program is already doing the private-garden
  version of our job at scale. The most likely early yes.
- **Mid-Atlantic** — our deepest plant list and Pollinator Pathway's
  concentration. Also the hardest crowd, which is useful early.
- **Ireland** — one coordinating body (the All-Ireland Pollinator Plan) instead
  of twelve, a brand-new 2026–2030 plan looking for tools, and council
  Biodiversity Officers who can act on public land.

France should wait until there's a French speaker on the thread, not because the
app can't — it does — but because a translated cold email reads as one.

## What to say

Short. The pitch is four facts:

- Free, no account, no ads, works offline.
- Region-specific: native means native *here*, and we say where the claim comes
  from.
- Wildlife-led: every plant carries the caterpillars and birds it feeds, counted
  from published research.
- We got things wrong and want to know which.

### Draft — chapter programs or horticulture chair

> Subject: A free native plant tool for [region] — would you tell us what we got wrong?
>
> Hello [name],
>
> I built Indigene (https://indigene.app) — a free, ad-free tool that answers one
> question: what should I plant in this spot? It's mobile-first, works offline,
> and asks for nothing, no account included.
>
> We cover [region] with [n] species. Native status comes from [source], and
> every plant shows the caterpillars and birds it actually supports.
>
> I'd rather hear from you than about you: would you look at our [region] list
> and tell me what's wrong with it? Your chapter knows this flora better than our
> sources do, and I'd fix what you flag.
>
> If it turns out to be useful afterwards, I'd love to talk about pointing new
> gardeners your way — they need a plant list and a plant sale, and we only do
> one of those.
>
> [name]

### Draft — French, for Tela Botanica / CBN / LPO

> Objet : Indigene — un outil libre sur les plantes indigènes, et une demande de relecture
>
> Bonjour [nom],
>

Personalize both. A template that reads like a template gets the reply a
template deserves.

## Be straight about the overlap

CNPS runs Calscape. The Institute for Regional Conservation runs *Natives For
Your Neighborhood*. The All-Ireland Pollinator Plan publishes plant lists. Each
of them will notice the resemblance within thirty seconds, so say it first:
Indigene is mobile-first, offline, free, wildlife-led and bilingual, and it
doesn't try to be a flora. Volunteering the comparison reads as confidence.
Omitting it reads as the other thing.

## The longer game: gardeners who become spokespeople

The ambition is a private native gardener who ends up arguing for native
plantings at their town's parks department. That ladder already exists in
places; we don't have to invent it, only to know where the rungs are.

| Rung | Where it already exists |
|---|---|
| Plants natives in their own yard | Us |
| Gets the yard recognized | Backyard Habitat Certification (Portland), NWF Certified Wildlife Habitat, Refuges LPO (France) |
| Shows the yard to strangers | Theodore Payne Garden Tour (LA), chapter garden tours everywhere |
| Joins a local group | Society chapters, Wild Ones, Irish Wildlife Trust branches |
| Changes a street or a town | Pollinator Pathway (300+ US towns), Tidy Towns biodiversity category (Ireland), council Biodiversity Officers |
| Changes an ordinance | NWF Mayors' Monarch Pledge — its actions include rewriting municipal landscaping and weed ordinances to require natives |

The gap Indigene sits in is the first rung, and the rung after it is where
people fall off: somebody plants six natives, likes it, and then has nowhere
obvious to go. Every organization in the directory is that next step.

**The app now says so.** Each region page ends with the groups whose ground it
is — `app/src/data/societies.ts`, three to five per region, chosen for the
region rather than the country. That changes the ask in the letters above: we
are no longer asking a chapter to consider a tool, we are telling them their
own region page already points readers at them.

Two are missing and should be added once somebody can load them: the **Native
Plant Society of British Columbia** (its addresses disagree — `npsbc.ca`, a
`vcn.bc.ca` path and a WordPress mirror — and none could be loaded to settle
which is live) and **Forest & Bird** for Auckland. A live page is the bar for a
link we put in front of a reader.

## Rules

- **Published organizational contacts only.** The address a group prints on its
  own contact page. No scraping rosters, no personal addresses, no LinkedIn
  archaeology.
- **One message, then let it go.** One follow-up after three weeks at most.
- **Never imply endorsement we don't have.** A chapter that replied is not a
  partner, and a partner is not an endorsement.
- **Write to a person, once.** No bcc lists, no mail merge.
