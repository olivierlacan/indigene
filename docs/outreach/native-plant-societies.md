# Native plant societies, region by region

A working directory of the organizations whose members are Indigene's audience,
organized against the twelve regions the app actually covers. Nothing here is
in the app; this is a contact list for reaching out.

**Researched 2026-09-20.** Volunteer organizations change officers every year or
two, so every entry below gives the *role* to ask for and the page that names
the current holder, rather than a name that will be wrong by spring. Pull the
name off the linked page the day you send. Where an organization publishes a
role-based address (`info@`, `president@`), that address is the durable one and
is quoted.

**Only publicly published organizational contacts are listed** — the addresses
these groups print on their own "contact us" pages. No personal addresses, no
scraped rosters.

## United States

### Mid-Atlantic / Northeast Piedmont

Indigene's box runs from central Virginia to southern New England and from the
coast to the Appalachian ridges — so this one region spans a dozen state
societies. They are separate organizations, not chapters of one body.

| Organization | Covers | Ask for | Published contact |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Pennsylvania Native Plant Society](https://www.panativeplantsociety.org/) | PA, several local chapters | President or the chapter chair | `info@panativeplantsociety.org` · [board](https://www.panativeplantsociety.org/board-of-directors.html) |
| [Native Plant Society of New Jersey](https://npsnj.org/) | NJ, ~1,600 members, county chapters | Chapter chair (Essex, Hudson, Bergen-Passaic, SouthWest and others) | site contact form |
| [Delaware Native Plant Society](https://delawarenativeplants.org/) | DE | President | site contact form |
| [Maryland Native Plant Society](https://mdflora.org/) | MD + DC | President or Programs chair | `info@mdflora.org` · [board](https://mdflora.org/2025Board) |
| [Virginia Native Plant Society](https://vnps.org/) | VA, ~16 chapters | Chapter president — Potowmack (northern VA), Piedmont, Jefferson (Charlottesville) are the ones inside our box | [chapter directory](https://vnps.org/chapters/) |
| [West Virginia Native Plant Society](https://www.wvnps.org/) | WV | President | site contact form |
| [Native Plant Trust](https://nativeplanttrust.org/) | All New England; the oldest plant conservation organization in the US (1900) | Education or Public Programs staff — this one has paid staff, so it is a partnership conversation, not a volunteer one | site contact form |
| [New York Flora Association](https://www.nyflora.org/) | NY state | Editor / outreach | site contact form |
| [Long Island Native Plant Initiative](https://linpi.org/) | Long Island; local-provenance seed | Director | site contact form |

| [Connecticut Botanical Society](https://www.ct-botanical-society.org/) | CT | Programs chair | site contact form |
| [New England Botanical Society](https://rhodora.org/) | New England, academic-leaning | Council | site contact form |

**The municipal lever here is [Pollinator Pathway](https://www.pollinator-pathway.org/)** — 300+ towns
running pesticide-free native corridors across public and private land,
concentrated in Connecticut, New York and New Jersey. It is the closest existing
thing to the spokesperson idea, and each town pathway has a named local
organizer.

### Northern Lower Michigan

| Organization | Covers | Ask for | Published contact |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Michigan Botanical Society](https://michiganbotanicalsociety.org/) | MI, five chapters (Great Lakes, Huron Valley, Southeastern, Southwestern, White Pine); founded 1941 | Chapter president. The **Great Lakes chapter** is the online/statewide one and is the right door for Petoskey and Traverse City — the other four meet downstate | [contacts](https://michiganbotanicalsociety.org/contacts) |
| [Wildflower Association of Michigan](https://wildflowersmich.org/) | MI; runs the annual Michigan Native Plant Conference | Board, or the conference organizer | site contact form |

Northern Lower Michigan has no society of its own. The regional land
conservancies around Little Traverse Bay and Grand Traverse Bay run the native
plant programming instead, and are worth approaching directly.

### Pacific Northwest

| Organization | Covers | Ask for | Published contact |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Washington Native Plant Society](https://www.wnps.org/) | WA, twelve chapters | Chapter president. Inside our box (west of the Cascade crest): **Central Puget Sound**, **South Sound** (Pierce/Thurston), **Salal** (north Snohomish/Skagit/Island), **Koma Kulshan** (Whatcom), **Olympic Peninsula**, **San Juan Islands** | [chapters](https://www.wnps.org/chapters) |
| [Native Plant Society of Oregon](https://www.npsoregon.org/wp/chapters/) | OR, ~11 chapters | Chapter president; the addresses follow a pattern — `em_president@npsoregon.org` (Emerald/Eugene), `ch_president@npsoregon.org` (Cheahmill). West of the Cascades: **Portland**, **Cheahmill**, **Willamette Valley**, **Corvallis**, **Emerald**, **Umpqua Valley**, **Siskiyou**, **South Coast** |[chapters](https://www.npsoregon.org/wp/chapters/) |
| [Backyard Habitat Certification Program](https://backyardhabitats.org/) | Portland metro (Multnomah, Clackamas, Washington, Clark counties) | Program staff at Bird Alliance of Oregon or Columbia Land Trust | site contact form |

The Backyard Habitat program is the single best-matched partner in this
directory: 14,500+ certified private yards, a site-assessment visit, native
plant discounts and a plaque on the fence. It is what Indigene's users are
already trying to do, run at scale, by staff.

### Southern California

One organization, six chapters. The [California Native Plant
Society](https://www.cnps.org/) has ~10,000 members across 35 chapters;
`membership@cnps.org` reaches the state office, and each chapter has its own
board page.

- [Los Angeles–Santa Monica Mountains](https://chapters.cnps.org/lasmm/)
- [South Coast](https://chapters.cnps.org/southcoast/) — LA / Long Beach, plus Catalina and San Clemente
- [San Gabriel Mountains](https://chapters.cnps.org/sgm/)
- [Orange County](https://chapters.cnps.org/oc/)
- [San Diego](https://chapters.cnps.org/sandiego/)
- [Channel Islands](https://chapters.cnps.org/channelislands/) — Santa Barbara / Ventura
- [Riverside–San Bernardino](https://riverside-sanbernardino.cnps.org/) — mostly outside our box, which stops at the mountain crest

Ask for the **Horticulture chair** or **Programs chair** rather than the
president: they are the ones who field "what do I plant" questions and book
speakers.

Also here: the [Theodore Payne Foundation](https://theodorepayne.org/), whose
annual [Native Plant Garden Tour](https://nativeplantgardentour.org/) opens
private gardens that are at least 50% native across greater Los Angeles. Every
garden on that tour is a private gardener who already became a public example —
the exact thing we want to encourage.

### Central California Coast

Same organization, ten chapters over our box:

- [Yerba Buena](https://cnps-yerbabuena.org/) — San Francisco
- [East Bay](https://ebcnps.org/) — Alameda & Contra Costa
- [Marin](https://cnpsmarin.org/)
- [Santa Clara Valley](https://www.cnps-scv.org/) — Santa Clara & southern San Mateo
- [Santa Cruz](https://chapters.cnps.org/santacruz/)
- [Monterey Bay](https://chapters.cnps.org/montereybay/)
- [Milo Baker](https://chapters.cnps.org/milobaker) — Sonoma
- [Napa Valley](https://chapters.cnps.org/napa/)
- [Willis Linn Jepson](https://www.cnps.org/about-cnps/chapters/jepson/) — Solano
- [San Luis Obispo](https://cnpsslo.org/) — just south of our edge

**Note the overlap:** CNPS runs [Calscape](https://calscape.org/), which answers
much the same question Indigene does for California. Approach them as a peer
with a different shape — mobile-first, offline, wildlife-led, and free — not as
a replacement. Being straight about that will land better than pretending not
to notice.

### North & Central Florida

The [Florida Native Plant Society](https://www.fnps.org/) has 30+ chapters;
`info@fnps.org`, (321) 271-6702, and a [chapter
directory](https://www.fnps.org/chapters) with meeting times. Chapters inside
this region:

- **Panhandle** — Longleaf Pine (Pensacola), Sweetbay (Panama City), Magnolia (Tallahassee), Sarracenia (Wakulla & Franklin)
- **Northeast** — Ixia (Jacksonville), Beluthahatchee (Fruit Cove), Pawpaw (Daytona)
- **North Central** — Paynes Prairie (Gainesville), Marion Big Scrub, Citrus, Hernando, The Villages
- **Central** — Tarflower (Winter Park), Cuplet Fern (Sanford), Passionflower (Clermont), Pine Lily (St. Cloud), Lake Beautyberry (Mount Dora), Heartland (Lakeland), Highlands
- **West Coast** — Pinellas, Suncoast, Serenoa (Sarasota/Manatee), Nature Coast
- **East Coast** — Sea Rocket (Titusville), Conradina (Melbourne), Eugenia (Vero Beach)

Also [Florida Wildflower Foundation](https://www.flawildflowers.org/), which
funds roadside and public-land wildflower plantings — a direct line to the
"public land, not just my yard" ambition.

### South Florida

FNPS again, south of Lake Okeechobee:

- Dade (Pinecrest) — also the nearest chapter to the Keys
- Broward County (Dania Beach)
- Palm Beach County (West Palm Beach)
- Martin County (Stuart)
- [Coccoloba](https://www.fnpscoccoloba.org/) (Lee County) and Naples (Collier)
- Mangrove (Englewood)

Also the [Institute for Regional Conservation](https://regionalconservation.org/),
whose *Natives For Your Neighborhood* gives South Florida plant lists by ZIP
code. Same overlap note as Calscape: a peer, not a competitor.

## France

**There is no French equivalent of a "native plant society."** Nobody there
organizes amateur gardeners around planting indigenous species the way CNPS or
FNPS do. The function is split four ways, and each piece is a different
conversation.

| What it does | Who | Why they matter to us |
|---|---|---|
| The botanical network | [Tela Botanica](https://www.tela-botanica.org/) (Montpellier) — tens of thousands of members, amateur and professional | The single best entry point in France. Runs [Sauvages de ma rue](https://www.tela-botanica.org/projets/sauvages-de-ma-rue/) with the Muséum national d'Histoire naturelle: citizens identify wild plants in city streets. An audience already doing our thing, with a newsletter that reaches them |
| The scientific authority | The [Conservatoires botaniques nationaux](https://www.fcbn.fr/) — 14 of them, [directory here](https://www.fcbn.fr/annuaire) | They decide what counts as native where. Per region: **CBN de Brest**, **CBN Sud-Atlantique** and **CBN du Bassin parisien** (Atlantic); **CBN de Franche-Comté** and **Bassin parisien** (Continental); **CBN méditerranéen de Porquerolles** and **CBN de Corse** (Mediterranean); **CBN alpin** at Gap-Charance (Alps). Approach as a source-correctness conversation, not a marketing one |
| Where to buy local-provenance plants | [Végétal local](https://www.ofb.gouv.fr/vegetal-local) — an OFB collective mark, run with the CBN network, Afac-Agroforesteries and Plante & Cité | The answer to "fine, but where do I get it?" A labelled nursery list we could point French users at |
| The gardeners | [LPO](https://www.lpo.fr/s-engager-a-nos-cotes/creer-un-refuge-lpo) — the **Refuges LPO** network is France's largest network of wildlife gardens; also Noé's *Jardins de Noé* | Closest thing to our actual user base. A Refuge LPO owner is a French private gardener who has already signed a charter about their own land |
| The municipal lever | [Plante & Cité](https://www.plante-et-cite.fr/) — the applied-research body for municipal green space | The route to city parks and public plantings, which is where the "commercial land and city parks" ambition actually lives in France |

Two more federations worth knowing: [France Nature
Environnement](https://fne.asso.fr/) (a federation of thousands of local
environmental associations) and the [Conservatoires d'espaces
naturels](https://reseau-cen.org/) network.

The [Société botanique de France](https://www.societebotaniquedefrance.fr/) is
the learned society — useful for credibility, not for reaching gardeners.

**Write to all of these in French.** Indigene already speaks it; a French-first
approach is the whole credibility of the outreach.

## Ireland

Ireland has no native plant society either, but it has something better: one
coordinating body everyone already follows.

| Organization | What it is | Ask for |
|---|---|---|
| [National Biodiversity Data Centre](https://biodiversityireland.ie/) | Runs the [All-Ireland Pollinator Plan](https://pollinators.ie/) — the 2026–2030 edition is just out. Gardens, councils, schools, farms and businesses all take actions under it | Pollinator Plan project officer |
| [BSBI Ireland](https://bsbi.org/ireland) | Botanical Society of Britain and Ireland — vice-county recorders, the people who know the actual flora | Irish Officer |
| [Irish Wildlife Trust](https://iwt.ie/) | Branches and volunteers nationwide, magazine, events | Branch organizer or communications |
| [Native Woodland Trust](https://www.nativewoodlandtrust.ie/) | 11 reserves, volunteer planting days | Trustee or volunteer coordinator |
| [Hometree](https://www.hometree.ie/) | Permanent native woodland in the west | Engagement lead |
| [Crann](https://crann.ie/) | Trees and hedgerows, members countrywide | Secretary |
| [An Taisce](https://www.antaisce.org/) | National Trust for Ireland; runs Green Schools | Green Schools / biodiversity |

**The municipal lever here is [Tidy Towns](https://www.tidytowns.ie/)**, which
has a biodiversity category every town competes in, and the Local Authority
**Heritage Officers** and **Biodiversity Officers** — a named council employee
in most counties whose job is exactly the rewilding-public-land conversation.
That is a shorter path from private gardener to public land than anything in
the US list.

## National, all US regions at once

| Organization | Reach | Why |
|---|---|---|
| [Wild Ones](https://wildones.org/chapters/) | 91 chapters and 29 seedling chapters in 34 states | The native *landscaping* organization, as opposed to the botanizing ones. Members are homeowners converting lawns — our users exactly. One conversation with the national office covers every US region |
| [Homegrown National Park](https://homegrownnationalpark.org/) | Doug Tallamy's movement; a national map of registered private plantings | The idea Indigene's wildlife pages are built on. They run a [partner program](https://homegrownnationalpark.org/partner/) |
| [Xerces Society](https://www.xerces.org/) | Invertebrate conservation, HQ in Portland | Owns the pollinator-habitat conversation; publishes regional plant lists |
| [National Wildlife Federation](https://www.nwf.org/MayorsMonarchPledge) | Certified Wildlife Habitat, and the **Mayors' Monarch Pledge** — 400+ mayors, with pledge actions that include rewriting city landscaping and weed ordinances to require native plants | The readymade mechanism for the city-parks ambition. A gardener who wants to change their town's plantings can point their mayor at an existing pledge instead of inventing one |
| [Pollinator Partnership](https://www.pollinator.org/) | Ecoregional planting guides | Their guides are keyed to the same EPA ecoregions Indigene uses |
| [North American Native Plant Society](https://nanps.org/native-plant-societies/) | Continental | Maintains the canonical [list of societies](https://nanps.org/native-plant-societies/) — the place to check this directory against when it goes stale |
